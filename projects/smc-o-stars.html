O-Type Stars in the Small Magellanic Cloud

Data integration, uncertainty modelling, and stellar population analysis of approximately 450 massive stars.

Home Projects GitHub

Overview

This project formed my final-year MPhys research project at the University of Sheffield. I constructed and analysed a catalogue of approximately 450 O-type stars in the Small Magellanic Cloud by combining multiple astronomical datasets.

The work involved catalogue cross-matching, photometric calibration, uncertainty propagation, Monte Carlo methods, stellar evolutionary model comparison, and population-level statistical analysis.

Research Questions

  • How can heterogeneous astronomical catalogues be combined into a consistent dataset?
  • What are the luminosities, masses, ages, and spatial properties of the O-star population?
  • How strongly do different evolutionary model grids affect the inferred stellar parameters?
  • How does the observed mass distribution compare with a standard initial mass function?

Methodology

  1. Cross-matched stellar catalogues and resolved inconsistent entries.
  2. Combined spectral classifications with optical photometry.
  3. Applied luminosity-class-dependent temperature and bolometric correction calibrations.
  4. Derived stellar luminosities from photometric and distance information.
  5. Propagated uncertainties using Monte Carlo simulations.
  6. Compared derived parameters with stellar evolutionary models to estimate masses and ages.
  7. Analysed HR diagrams, mass functions, age distributions, and spatial structure.

Key Results

  • Produced a cleaned and internally consistent catalogue of approximately 450 O-type stars.
  • Derived stellar luminosities with asymmetric propagated uncertainties.
  • Estimated masses and ages using multiple stellar evolutionary model grids.
  • Identified differences between model grids in the inferred age distribution and high-mass population.
  • Compared the observed mass distribution with a Salpeter-like initial mass function.
  • Mapped the O-star population across the Small Magellanic Cloud.
